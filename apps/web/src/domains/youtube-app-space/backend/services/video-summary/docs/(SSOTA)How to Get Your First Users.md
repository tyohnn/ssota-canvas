## 🧭 How Do You Acquire Your First Real Users When Launching a New Product?
**Most people aren't early adopters**, so startups must search for rare individuals who love new products or have burning problems. **Build a minimum evolvable product (MEP)** that survives initial contact and adapts based on early user feedback.

## 🚀 Core Strategies for Finding and Evolving with Early Adopters
- **Search for Gustafs and Ankits**: Target people eager to try startups or desperate for solutions via <mark>personal outreach</mark>.
- **Charge real money early**: Paying customers provide <mark>sharper feedback</mark> than free users.
- **Launch early and experiment fast**: Engineer wide discovery while studying users like anthropologists and ignoring churn.

Readers gain a **search-oriented mindset** over persuasion, freeing them to iterate rapidly without perfection pressure. This unlocks **path-dependent evolution**, turning amoeba-like products into mature successes like Tesla's lineup.

# 🎞️ Blueprint for Startup Launch: Hunt Early Users, Build Evolvable Products, and Let Feedback Drive Evolution

## 1. Rarity of Early Adopters
1. Most people use <mark>zero products</mark> as one of the first 10 users.
2. Almost no one wants to be a startup's <mark>first paying customer</mark>.
3. Great products still find a few willing to leap.

## 2. Minimum Evolvable Product Over MVP
1. Earliest version only needs to <mark>survive contact</mark> with tiny groups.
2. Build something that can <mark>evolve</mark>, not the final form.
3. YC emphasizes <mark>MEP</mark> to respond to market pressures.

## 3. Early Adopter Archetype: Product Enthusiasts
1. People like Gustaf at Airbnb <mark>love trying startups</mark> and evangelizing them.
2. They introduce products into companies for broader reach.

## 4. Early Adopter Archetype: Burning Problems
1. Desperate users try anything promising relief, ignoring size or reputation.
2. Example: Paid unproven startup for <mark>inference API billing</mark> within 3 days as their first customer.

## 5. First Users as Search Problem
1. Focus on finding Gustafs/Ankits, not persuading masses.
2. <mark>Counterintuitive implications</mark> follow from this mindset.

## 6. Implication: Charge Real Money Early
1. Early adopters with needs are <mark>not price sensitive</mark>.
2. Paying users give <mark>sharper feedback</mark> than free ones.
3. Angry high-paying customers motivate faster fixes.

## 7. Implication: Targeted Personal Outreach
1. Skip billboards; use <mark>cold emails or door knocks</mark>.
2. Methods differ from reaching normal customers.

## 8. Implication: Launch Early
1. YC doctrine: Create <mark>wide surface area</mark> for discovery.
2. Unknown early user profiles demand broad exposure.

### 8.1 Benefits of Early Launch
1. Enables quick <mark>evolutionary search</mark> through possibilities.
   a. Test pricing, pages, onboarding, features constantly.

## 9. Implication: Study Early Users Closely
1. Observe like anthropologists: <mark>decision-making and trust factors</mark>.
2. Understand their thinking to refine product.

## 10. Implication: Experiment Fast, Ignore Churn
1. Run constant tests while building personal relationships.
2. Churn is fine; <mark>plenty more undiscovered users exist</mark>.
3. Startups avoid big-company headline risks from bad experiments.

## 11. Consumer vs. Enterprise Realities in AI Era
1. Personal software spend is tiny (~<mark>$150/month</mark>).
2. Corporate tools cost far more individually.
3. AI costs push founders to <mark>proumers, businesses, high-value users</mark> like doctors.

## 12. Early Users Steer Product Evolution
1. Users don't just feedback; they <mark>shape direction</mark>.
2. Path-dependent like <mark>phylogenetic tree</mark>: amoeba to complex organisms.

## 13. Tesla Roadster as Evolutionary Amoeba
1. High-margin $150k car tested <mark>crazy early adopters</mark>.
2. Funded capex while revealing preferences.
3. Model Y's <mark>acceleration over comfort</mark> reflects Roadster adopters' priorities.

### 13.1 Path Dependence in Product Design
1. Mass-market cars prioritize tech/speed due to <mark>early signals</mark>.
   a. Vacuum design likely slower, comfier.
2. Alternate adopters (e.g., plush seekers) would yield different cars.

## 14. YC's Evolutionary Algorithm
1. Start simple, adapt to <mark>early pressures</mark>.
2. Frees founders: product changes a lot based on beginnings.

## Conclusion: Build to Evolve with First Users
1. Prioritize <mark>MEP and user search</mark> for survival-to-maturity path.
2. Early choices determine final form; <mark>launch, learn, adapt relentlessly</mark>.
